Analysis / Taxonomy Comparison / July 2026

Two Bodies of Knowledge
Side-by-Side Analysis

Standard field taxonomy (sourced from Stanford HAI, NVIDIA, McKinsey, Gartner) vs. personal practitioner taxonomy (betodon96). Evaluated on relevance and comprehensiveness for the target user: independent advisor at the intersection of SE, business strategy, and AI.

Taxonomy A
Standard Field Taxonomy
Derived from authoritative institutional sources. Describes the AI landscape as the research and industry community has organized it. Layered, comprehensive, oriented toward "what AI is."
Layer 01: Foundational Subfields (ML, DL, RL, NLP, CV, Multimodal)
Layer 02: Applied Domains (GenAI, Agents, MLOps, Enterprise AI, Safety, AI for Science)
Layer 03: Emerging Branches (Reasoning, World Models, Embodied AI, Neuromorphic)
Layer 04: Business & Organizational (Strategy, Governance, Economics)
Taxonomy B
Personal Practitioner Taxonomy
Built from the practitioner's perspective. Organized around what to DO with AI, not what AI IS. Non-MECE by design — overlap zones are productive research territory.
Domain 01: Engineering AI Systems (agents, context, pipelines, eval, MCP)
Domain 02: Enterprise AI (strategy, deployment, governance, change)
Domain 03: Eval Sets (horizontal — applies to all other domains)
Domain 04: Engineering Systems through AI (SE augmentation, MBSE, artifact-free AI)

03 Synthesis & Research Sprint Recommendations
The compass and the map
Analytical conclusion

The standard taxonomy is a map of the territory — comprehensive, sourced, useful for explaining the AI landscape to others and tracking where the frontier is moving. The personal taxonomy is a compass for navigating it — focused, practitioner-oriented, built around what to do rather than what exists. Both are correct at what they do. The mistake would be to treat them as alternatives.

The personal taxonomy has three genuine innovations that the field taxonomy lacks: (1) Context & memory engineering as a first-class domain, (2) Eval sets as a horizontal discipline rather than a buried sub-technique, and (3) Engineering Systems through AI as a named paradigm. These are not gaps in the standard taxonomy from laziness — they are gaps because the standard taxonomy describes research fields, not practitioner knowledge domains. Your taxonomy is organized around a different question.

The personal taxonomy's weak spot is temporal awareness — it has no structure for tracking emerging branches (world models, reasoning models, embodied AI) that are not yet relevant today but will be in 2–3 years. A simple "watch list" connection to the standard taxonomy's Layer 03 closes this gap without requiring active research investment now.
